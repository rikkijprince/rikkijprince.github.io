# Why I Abandoned the AI-Only Approach

When I began developing Hybrid English, my original ambition was quite different from the system you see today.

I set out to create a **completely automated language-learning system**. At the time, systems of this kind did not yet exist in the form I had in mind. The idea was to use artificial intelligence to provide the learner with a complete learning environment: conversation, practice, analysis, correction, adaptation and feedback, without the need for a human teacher.

The attraction was obvious. A machine could be available whenever the learner wanted to practise. It could repeat an exercise indefinitely without becoming bored or impatient. It could analyse enormous quantities of language and identify patterns that would be difficult for an individual teacher to track over a long period. And, as the technology developed, it became possible to make the interaction increasingly natural.

So I pursued the idea seriously.

But the more I tested it, the more I became dissatisfied with the result.

The problem was not that the AI was insufficiently knowledgeable. Quite the opposite. It could draw on an enormous database of language and information. The problem was something much more difficult to reproduce: **the originality that comes from a human being speaking from his or her own life experience.**

A teacher who has lived through particular situations can bring something unexpected to a conversation. They can tell a story that is genuinely their own, make an observation based on something that actually happened to them, recognise an emotional reaction, or understand why a particular learner is struggling at a particular moment. They can respond not simply to the words a student has produced, but to the person producing them.

A trained system can generate an extraordinarily convincing response. But convincing is not the same as having lived the experience behind it.

That distinction became increasingly important to me.

I realised that I had been asking the wrong question. The objective did not need to be to **replace the teacher with AI**. The more interesting question was: **what happens if we stop asking either one to do what the other does better?**

That led me to the hybrid model.

## The best of both worlds

Hybrid English is therefore not the result of abandoning artificial intelligence. It is the result of deciding where artificial intelligence is genuinely useful — and where a human being remains irreplaceable.

The AI provides what machines do exceptionally well. It has access to an enormous body of language and knowledge. It can practise with a learner repeatedly and patiently. It does not get tired of answering questions or asking for another attempt. It can analyse a learner's speech, identify recurring patterns and provide a level of continuous data that would be extremely difficult for a human teacher to maintain manually.

The human teacher provides something different.

A teacher brings **life experience, judgement, originality and real empathy**. A teacher can introduce something that was not simply retrieved from a database: a personal perspective, an unexpected connection, a memory, a story, or an understanding of what another human being is experiencing.

And the teacher can use the information generated by the AI without being replaced by it. Instead of spending valuable time trying to observe and remember every detail of a student's performance, the teacher can use the accumulated evidence to decide where human intervention will have the greatest value.

That is the principle behind Hybrid English 5.0.

I no longer believe that the best language-learning system is one in which the human teacher disappears. Nor do I believe that the best use of a teacher's time is to do manually what an AI system can do continuously and patiently.

The solution, in my view, is to combine the two.

**AI provides the scale, patience, information and analysis. The human teacher provides the experience, judgement and empathy.**

The result is not a compromise between two imperfect alternatives. It is an attempt to give each one the job it is best equipped to do.

That is why I abandoned my original ambition of creating a completely automated language-learning system — and why I chose instead to build Hybrid English.
